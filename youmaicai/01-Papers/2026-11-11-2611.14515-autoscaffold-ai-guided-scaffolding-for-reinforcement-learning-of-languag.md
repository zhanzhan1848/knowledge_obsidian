## 核心要点
1. 指出 **RLVR 产生丰富训练经验，但结果奖励把每次尝试压成单个数字**，浪费了大部分信息。
2. 训练期脚手架（策略或提示）可把信息转成指导，但其设计**主要由人手工完成且高度领域特定**。
3. **AutoScaffold** 是统一的 **AI-for-AI** 框架：冻结教师**定期检视训练经验、诊断瓶颈、设计两类脚手架**（跨任务类的策略 / 单个困难任务的提示），**随学生进步调整内容与支持量**，**评测时移除**脚手架。
4. 四个领域（ALFWorld、检索式 QA、数学推理、GPU kernel 生成）上，**无脚手架评测**时学生全面超过对应 RL 基线（ALFWorld 成功率 **+7.2 点**，QA 平均 EM **+4.0 点**）。
5. GPU kernel 上「正确且快于 PyTorch 参考」的任务比例最高 **+5.0 点**；数学上以**约 1/3 的学生训练步数**超过 QuestA 报告的准确率。
6. 分析显示教师在每个任务类识别出**不同的失败**，且随学生进步**把脚手架从逐步工作流转向针对性修复**；修订后的脚手架比沿用旧脚手架或不用脚手架学到更多。

## 摘要（原文）
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) produces rich training experience, yet outcome
rewards reduce each attempt to a single number and leave most of this information unused. Training-
time scaffolds, such as strategies or hints added to the prompt during training, offer a natural way
to turn this information into guidance, but their design and adaptation are largely left to humans
and tailored to specific domains. We introduce AutoScaffold, a unified AI-for-AI framework that
turns a student's training experience into adaptive scaffolds, with minimal domain-specific
information and no human intervention during training. A frozen teacher periodically examines this
experience, diagnoses bottlenecks, and designs scaffolds in two forms: (i) strategies shared across
a class of tasks and (ii) hints for individual hard tasks, adjusting the content and amount of
support as the student learns. The scaffolds are removed at evaluation. We apply the same framework
to ALFWorld, search-based QA, mathematical reasoning, and GPU kernel generation. When evaluated
without scaffolds, students trained with AutoScaffold outperform those trained with the
corresponding RL baselines in all four domains, with gains of 7.2 points in ALFWorld success and 4.0
points in average QA exact match. On GPU kernel generation, AutoScaffold increases the fraction of
tasks with a correct kernel that runs faster than the PyTorch reference by up to 5.0 points. On
math, our student surpasses QuestA's reported accuracy using approximately one-third as many student
training steps. Analyses show that the teacher identifies a different failure in each task class and
shifts its scaffolds from step-by-step workflows to targeted fixes as the student improves, and that
its revised scaffolds help the student learn more than keeping the previous scaffold or using no
scaffold.
