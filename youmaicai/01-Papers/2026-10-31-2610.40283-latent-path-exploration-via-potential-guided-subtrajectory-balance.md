## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Continuous latent reasoning enables language models to perform intermediate computation without
generating explicit reasoning traces. Advances in stochastic latent reasoning allow multiple paths
to be sampled in continuous space, but stochasticity alone does not ensure effective exploration. A
key challenge is guiding stochastic transitions between intermediate latent states toward useful
reasoning paths. We introduce SubLAT, which couples a relative answer potential with Subtrajectory
Balance to learn stochastic latent exploration. The relative potential evaluates the answer support
of sampled states against replacement states, with a correctness signal at the terminal state.
Subtrajectory Balance relates potential differences to forward and backward transition densities
across subtrajectories of different lengths. The model is jointly trained with answer supervision,
CoT supervision, and representation distillation. Experiments on arithmetic and deductive reasoning
show increasing solution coverage with larger sampling budgets across in-domain and out-of-domain
benchmarks, as well as improvements on several structural OOD benchmarks. Trajectory analyses reveal
progressive divergence among sampled latent paths and higher mean relative potentials for correct
trajectories across arithmetic benchmarks.
