## 核心要点
1. 在 **Chatbot Arena** 上用 **1,260,967 次盲投**（2023 年 5 月–2024 年 8 月）分离「模型变差」与「评判者变严」两种可能；通过限定模型版本固定（厂商日期快照 + 固定开源权重）的配对，构造「同一对未变模型」的对照分析。
2. 关键结果：**「两个都不好」投票占比每月上升 1.71 个百分点**（均值 17.1%），2024 年为每月 1.91 点；在 2023 与 2024 均被高频观测的 **14 对模型中无一例外全部上升，从 15.5% 升至 23.3%**。而**排名不漂移**——2024 年强模型在决定性投票中的份额持平。
3. 稳健性覆盖同投票者同配对、不同加入队列、prompt 控制、各主要语言、界面改版等条件；漂移为**跨模型共享**（用其他模型配对估计的月度效应可从本模型配对中扣除该漂移）。平台整体上平均模型相对 2023 年平均预期得分 78%，但「两个都不好」比例最终回到起点附近——即**标准随品类进步而抬高，停滞即被判为退步**，这对 LLM 排行榜与用于训练模型的人类反馈数据都有影响。

## 摘要（原文）
Users often complain that AI assistants such as ChatGPT are getting worse. Some versions do change, but human
evaluation leaves a second possibility untested: the people judging may change. We separate the two on
Chatbot Arena, a public platform where people compare answers from two anonymous large language models
(LLMs) and pick the better one or say both are bad. We study 1,260,967 blind votes cast from May 2023 to
August 2024 on pairs of models whose versions are fixed: dated vendor snapshots and models with fixed open
weights. For the same pair of unchanged models, the share of votes saying "both are bad" rises by 1.71
percentage points a month on a mean of 17.1%, and by 1.91 points a month in 2024. In fourteen pairs
observed often in both 2023 and 2024, it rose in every one, from 15.5% to 23.3%. The ranking does not trend:
in 2024, the stronger model's share of decisive votes is flat. The rise holds within the same voter and on
the same pair, and across arrival cohorts from their first recorded vote. It holds with prompt controls, in
every major language, for dated vendor snapshots, open-weight models and models with stable answer length,
and between interface changes. The drift is shared across models: month effects estimated from other
models' pairs remove it from each model's own pairs. Platform-wide, the average model would be expected to
score 78% against the 2023 average, yet the share of "both are bad" votes ended near where it began. The
pattern fits a standard that follows the category's progress, so a product that stands still is judged as
declining. Absolute verdicts on AI drifted while relative comparisons held steady, an evaluation drift that
matters for LLM leaderboards and for the human feedback used to train models.
