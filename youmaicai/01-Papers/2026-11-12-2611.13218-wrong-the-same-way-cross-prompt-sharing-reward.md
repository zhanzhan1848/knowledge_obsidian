## 核心要点
1. 在**可精确计算完整响应分布的小规模受控环境**中，厘清 RLVR 与不完美验证器的两种相反说法：奖励攻击究竟是**从基座模型继承**，还是**被 RL 放大**。结论：在 **KL 正则最优解**处，RLVR 只是一个**过滤器**——重加权被接受的集合而不改变其组成，因此**攻击率被原样继承**（构成零模型/null model）。
2. 将偏离零模型的漂移分解为**各提示上奖励与经验切核（empirical tangent kernel）之间的协方差之和**：无参数共享时其他提示的项消失；**有共享时**，每个接受错误答案的提示都推高它、每个拒绝的提示通过基线把它压低，且与"模型在该提示上产生该错误答案的概率"成比例——**共享参数会把少数提示接受的泄漏压到基线以下，把多数提示接受的泄漏放大**。
3. 实证与启示：在 **Adam、SGD、采样式 GRPO** 的每个收敛学习率下，高普适性（universality）端的攻击率都高于低普适性端，但两端是否越过基线率**取决于步长**；**逐提示独立复制**参数即可消除这种依赖。方向由"错误答案的核加权概率有多少落在接受它的提示上"决定，这由**冻结的基座模型支撑集**所控制。验证器修复应针对**模型跨提示重复犯的错误**：两个移除等量错误的补丁，真实准确率可相差**高达 38 个百分点**。

## 摘要（原文）
Two accounts of how reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) interacts with an imperfect
verifier coexist: in one, reward hacking is inherited from the base model; in the other, RL amplifies
it. We study both in a controlled environment small enough to compute the entire response distribution
exactly. We vary the universality of a leak, or the fraction of prompts that accept the same wrong
response, and the verifier's static false-positive rate. At its KL-regularized optimum, RLVR acts as a
filter that reweights the accepted set without altering its composition, so the hack rate is inherited
exactly. Taking that as a null model, a known decomposition writes the drift from it as a sum over
prompts of covariances between reward and the empirical tangent kernel. The terms from other prompts
vanish without parameter sharing. With sharing, every prompt that accepts a wrong answer pushes it up
and every prompt that rejects it pushes it down through the baseline, in proportion to the model's
probability of producing it there. A shared-parameter policy thus drives a leak that few prompts accept
below the base rate and amplifies one that most prompts accept. At every convergent learning rate under
Adam, SGD, and sampled GRPO, hacking ends higher at high universality than at low, but whether either
end crosses the base rate depends on the step size. Independent per-prompt copies remove the
dependence. The direction of this push depends on neither the verifier's error rate nor universality
alone, but on how much of the model's kernel-weighted probability of a wrong answer falls on prompts
that accept it, which frozen base model controls support. Verifier fixes should target the mistakes the
model repeats across prompts: two patches that remove equally many errors differ by up to 38 points of
true accuracy. We propose this alignment as a hypothesis for why earlier studies disagree.
