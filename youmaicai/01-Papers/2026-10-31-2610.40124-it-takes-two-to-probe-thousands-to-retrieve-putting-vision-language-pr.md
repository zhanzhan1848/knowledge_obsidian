## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Vision--language models (VLMs) are tested for fine-grained understanding with image--text matching
probes, a model must prefer the correct image or caption over handcrafted negative. In retrieval,
the correct item must beat thousands of alternatives. We measure how these two evaluation methods
relate by using two probing benchmarks, \svo{} and SugarCrepe, for retrieval tasks. For 100 image
and caption queries we judge the top-10 retrievals from full dataset of six VLMs, with human
annotators and an LLM assessor. We than compare how well models rank pool of items on the same
queries, while changing only where the negatives comes from: the benchmark, a random draw, or the
judged-incorrect retrievals of other models. On \svo{} the accuracy of retrieving a positive over
handcrafted negatives is significantly higher than for retrieved ones (the correct item is preferred
in 85\% against 77\% of the time). Using pool of ten retrieved negatives leads to same accuracy as
when one uses pool of 200 to 430 random negatives. On SugarCrepe the order is reversed: its
handcrafted negatives are harder than the retrieved ones. Incomplete relevance labels explain most
of the low retrieval scores on \svo{}, where the top-ranked item is correct for 54--90\% of the
queries under human labels against 6--21\% under the benchmark's labels. However, we detect no
association between passing the probe for a query and retrieving a correct item first. The LLM
assessor o3 agrees with individual annotators ($κ$ 0.57--0.61) slightly less than they agree with
each other (0.64--0.68).
