## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Robotic navigation typically assumes a collision-free route to the goal exists; in cluttered
environments with movable obstacles, however, reaching the goal may require physical interaction.
Classical solutions encode these behaviors through geometric rules and task-specific logic, while
learning-based approaches often constrain the decision space to low-dimensional motion commands or
predefined action sets. We instead consider a broader decision space, jointly selecting the skill,
relevant object, and geometric target. Our language-conditioned task planner, fine-tuned with LoRA
on synthetic data, maps natural-language tasks and a structured scene representation to variable-
length sequences of parameterized actions, executed by low-level reinforcement-learning policies.
The planner learns the geometric relations underlying interactive navigation and outperforms a
classical planner and a prompted frontier model on interactive navigation tasks. Comparisons with
models trained from scratch indicate that composition does not require pretraining when the
representation is structured, whereas pretraining yields more reliable geometric targets. At the low
level, we introduce an end-to-end whole-body planar pushing policy that achieves a 93.3% success
rate in simulation, versus 83.5% for a hierarchical baseline, while reducing completion time by more
than 60%. On hardware, the hierarchy yields the correct skill composition in 44/45 trials and end-
to-end success in 34/45. Project website: https://anonymousprojects-dev.github.io/LLM-guided-
LocoManipulation-Quadrupeds/
