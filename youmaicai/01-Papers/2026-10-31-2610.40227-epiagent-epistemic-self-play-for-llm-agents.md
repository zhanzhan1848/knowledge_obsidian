## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Epistemic game theory explains an agent's strategic behavior via the beliefs it holds about others
and how these beliefs are updated as the interactions unfold. Modern LLM-based agents, however,
often lack an explicit mechanism for learning and updating such beliefs to guide decisions under
incomplete information. To bridge this gap, we propose EpiAgent, a framework that equips LLM-based
agents with an explicit, learnable epistemic layer, which maintains beliefs over others' private
information and combines these beliefs with the agent's available information into an epistemic
state for action selection. Concretely, the agent's policy is decomposed into two coupled stages:
Epistemic Update, which updates these beliefs from interaction histories, and Action Planning, which
selects actions conditioned on the updated epistemic state. By formulating their dependency as a bi-
level optimization problem, we introduce Epistemic Self-Play, an algorithm that alternately
optimizes the two stages through self-play, utilizing an intrinsic prediction reward to evaluate how
well inferred beliefs predict subsequent observations. Empirical results across both mixed and
competitive scenarios show that EpiAgent consistently outperforms prompting-based and RL-based
baselines. Further analyses reveal the benefits of dedicated epistemic training, improvements in
belief inference accuracy, and the contribution of learned beliefs to action planning. These results
demonstrate the value of an explicit, learnable epistemic layer for effective multi-agent
interactions.
