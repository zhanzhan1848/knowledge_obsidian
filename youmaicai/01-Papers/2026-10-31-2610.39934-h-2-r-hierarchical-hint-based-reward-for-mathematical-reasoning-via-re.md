## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards has shown great promise in enhancing mathematical
reasoning in large language models. However, existing approaches provide limited intermediate
supervision for complex, multi-step reasoning tasks. To bridge this gap, we propose
\textbf{Hierarchical Hint-based Reward}, a framework that leverages the intrinsic sub-question
structure of mathematical problems as structured intermediate conditioning signals during RLVR
training. Unlike chain-of-thought prompting, these hierarchical hints serve as logically grounded
intermediate milestones that effectively reduce the reasoning horizon toward the final answer. To
validate this framework, we introduce \textbf{HiMath}, a benchmark of hierarchically structured
mathematical problems. Across diverse LLMs, hierarchical hints consistently improve reasoning
performance even without task-specific training. When integrated into RL training via H$^2$R, they
boost Qwen2.5-Math-7B accuracy on HiMath from 30.4\% to 39.1\% using hierarchical hints, and up to
45.6\% with Oracle Hierarchical Hints. This pronounced improvement demonstrates that incorporating
reliable intermediate checkpoints into reward design is essential for sustaining complex
mathematical reasoning.
