## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Reasoning vision-language-action (VLA) policies write a reasoning trace and then decode the action
from it. A wrong trace yields a wrong action, and even a correct-looking trace can mislead the plan.
Existing remedies ask whether the reasoning is right. We ask how strongly it should steer. CoC-CFG
applies classifier-free guidance between the policy's context and the same context without the
reasoning span. One weight per Euler step attenuates or amplifies the reasoning, and w=1 recovers
the original policy. We treat the choice of this weight as a Markov decision process whose action
values are measured, not bootstrapped. With the expert frozen and the noise fixed, applying a weight
for one step and finishing the plan under the current controller returns that weight's value
exactly. Q-adaptive guidance fits these values with small critics and improves the controller
conservatively. In the Alpasim driving simulator it outperforms every fixed weight and online RL
(SAC, GRPO) in open loop, and in closed loop it passes 262 of 300 scenes, against 252 for the
original policy. On held-out frames of the Waymo end-to-end driving benchmark, its first three
stages outperform every fixed weight, four adaptive-guidance methods, SAC and GRPO. On the hidden
test set, a frozen Alpamayo 1.5 with rule-based camera and vehicle conversions and Q-adaptive
guidance sets a new state of the art among published methods: 8.135159 rater feedback score, against
8.079 for the best published method.
