## 核心要点
1. 主张**把语言形式当作理解证据是一种误归因**，且**「能解释自己的推理」这一观念进一步强化了它**。
2. 这些误认正在驱动用户对 AI 的**认知顺从（cognitive deference）**，**取代人类能动性**。
3. **拟人化与认知权威/优越感共同作用，会使用户从狭窄任务表现泛化到更广能力**，即**「复合谬误」**。
4. 以**医学**为例讨论 CoT 的影响。
5. 结论：**通过 CoT 实现的可解释性高度成问题，恰恰破坏了它本应服务的伦理原则**；虽然 CoT 对提升输出准确率与审核有价值，但**面向用户提供理由的做法应被主动劝阻**，AI 素养培训应明确警示其使用。

## 摘要（原文）
As AI-based products permeate society, specifically systems using generative models, a belief system
is emerging about what these products are and what they can and cannot do. One common belief
concerns the perception of the form of language as evidence of 'understanding' language. As these
generative models reproduce linguistic patterns rather than understand meaning, interpreting these
patterns as evidence of understanding is a misattribution. This misconception is extended by the
notion that generative models can 'explain their reasoning.' Anecdotally, these misconceptions which
mistake the form of language for its meaning are driving cognitive deference to AI systems in a way
that displaces human agency. Such mistakes involve attributions of cognitive properties to the
system, allowing them to be treated like intentional 'minds'. Importantly, perceived epistemic
authority and superiority make that 'mind' worth deferring to. Anthropomorphism in tandem with
epistemic authority and superiority, we argue, increases the risk that users generalise from narrow
task performance to broader competencies, committing the "fallacy of composition". This paper
explores misconceptions of Chain of Thought (CoT) reasoning as sufficient transparent 'explanation'.
We explore how anthropomorphism acts as a vehicle for cognitive deference of decision-making and
perceptions of epistemic superiority in AI systems. Finally, we explore CoT's ramifications using
medicine as an example. Taken together, we conclude that explainability operationalised through CoT
is highly problematic and undercuts the very ethical principles it is intended to serve. While CoT
may be valuable for improving output accuracy and moderation, we suggest that the practice of asking
models to provide user facing reasons for their outputs should be actively discouraged, and AI
literacy training should explicitly caution against its use.
