## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Multimodal large language models can recognize visual content yet struggle to reason reliably over
it across multiple steps. In counting, a model may see all objects but lose track of which ones it
has counted. We argue that such failures, commonly attributed to perception, reveal a reference gap:
the failure to bind language to the relevant visual entities, regions, or locations. Motivated by
the theory of visual routines, we test whether external references such as numbering can narrow this
gap. We introduce ReGap-Bench, a paired benchmark of five visual reasoning tasks in which the scene,
question, and answer are fixed and only the reference varies. Results show that task-matched
references improve accuracy by up to 25\%, while mismatched references provide little benefit or
even hurt performance. These findings identify the reference gap as a bottleneck distinct from
visual perception and show that constructing task-appropriate visual references can serve as a
training-free visual skill for improving multimodal reasoning.
