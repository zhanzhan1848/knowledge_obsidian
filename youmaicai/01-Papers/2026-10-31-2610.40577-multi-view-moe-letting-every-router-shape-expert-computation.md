## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) models expand parameter capacity by activating a small subset of
experts per token. Expert selection directly affects model capabilities under a fixed active-expert
budget. Dense evaluation of all experts reveals weak agreement between single-router rankings and
expert-utility evaluation rankings, especially in middle and deep layers. Richer signals for
selection and computation incur additional cost: selection based on internal expert activations
requires preliminary computation, while processing multiple full representations separately through
the same expert repeats its computation. We propose Multi-View MoE to improve expert selection with
multiple routers and reuse their preferences to modulate expert computation under a fixed expert
budget. Its Multi-Router submodule aggregates sigmoid scores from independently initialized routers.
These routers develop highly differentiated expert preferences during training, allowing experts
strongly favored by one router or jointly supported by several routers to be selected. Multi-View
further uses each selected expert's relative scores across routers to mix learned latent views into
its input, reusing routing preferences for both selection and computation. Each selected expert
executes one forward pass, preserving the active-expert count, expert FLOPs, and all-to-all
communication volume. On a 24-layer 10B-A1B model trained on 708B tokens, full Multi-View MoE
improves the nine-task downstream mean by 1.18 percentage points over the baseline. Multi-Router
alone yields a 0.6-percentage-point gain and improves the rank correlation between router scores and
evaluated expert utility, with relative increases of 8.1% globally and approximately 58% in the
layer with the lowest single-router alignment. Controlled mixing interventions show the model uses
input differences that depend on both the expert and the token.
