## 核心要点
1. 指出**仇恨言论、假新闻、恐怖主义等概念存在多个理论上站得住的竞争性定义**，且**未知模型是否真按定义标注**。
2. 方法：**同一批文本用不同定义各标注一次**，两次标注的分歧称为 **definition sensitivity**；对照是**改写定义但保持要求不变**，分歧构成 **paraphrase floor**。
3. 跨 **6 个概念、3 个 LLM**：**竞争性定义的切换总是比改写造成更多标注变化**——说明**标注依赖于所提示的定义**。
4. 因此**任何标注集都应与产生它的定义一起解读**。作者建议**同时报告定义敏感性与改写底线**。

## 摘要（原文）
In social science research, text annotation is a fundamental task that is traditionally a costly,
hard-to-scale manual process. Researchers have used large language models (LLMs) to perform
annotation by writing a definition of a concept into a prompt - a rule that determines whether a
label should be attributed to a text depending on its content. However, concepts such as hate
speech, fake news, and terrorism typically have several theoretically defensible competing
definitions. It is also unknown whether the model actually applied the definition to provide the
label or the label was based on the model's pretrained notion of the concept. To test this, we have
the model annotate the same texts twice, each time with a different definition. We call the
disagreement between the two sets of annotations definition sensitivity. As a control, we paraphrase
one definition but leave its requirements unchanged - the disagreement between the annotations with
the two wordings becomes the paraphrase floor. Across six concepts and three LLMs, a swap of
competing definitions always changes more annotations, on average, than a paraphrase. This shows
that the annotations depend on the prompted definition, so any set of annotations should be
interpreted together with the definition that produced it. The LLM annotator's definition
sensitivity and its paraphrase floor should both be reported in such work.
