## 技术方案

### 核心思想

As Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly participate in persistent social environments,
understanding their long-term social interaction capabilities becomes increasingly important. While
existing research has advanced long-term memory and conversational context retrieval, less attention
has been paid to whether agents can autonomously re-engage with previously inactive conversations.
In particular, remembering a past interaction does not necessarily mean that an agent can identify
when to revisit it, decide to return, or reconstruct the context needed for a coherent continuation.
To investigate this gap, we introduce agent social re-entry as a multi- dimensional evaluation
problem, distinguishing three complementary capabilities: Finding, Returning, and Reconstructing. We
develop a shared-snapshot evaluation framework that measures these dimensions independently,
allowing us to examine how conversational resurfacing and persistent memory relate to different
aspects of social re-entry. Evaluations across six LLM configurations show that resurfacing
substantially improves target conversation identification, while persistent memory is associated
with higher rates of autonomous target-directed replying. However, neither improvement is
accompanied by consistent gains in contextual reconstruction. These results highlight the
distinction between accessing previous conversations, choosing to re-engage, and maintaining
conversational continuity. Our framework provides a way to evaluate these capabilities separately
rather than relying on memory retrieval or conversational response quality alone.
