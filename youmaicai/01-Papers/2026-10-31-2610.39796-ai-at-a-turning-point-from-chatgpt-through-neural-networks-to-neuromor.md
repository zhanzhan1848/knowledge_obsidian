## 技术方案

### 核心思想

The tension between the established technologies of connectionist AI and their neuromorphic
challengers is attracting growing attention, above all because of the enormous energy demand of the
former and the low power consumption of the latter. Developing and running large AI models consumes
gigawatt- to terawatt-hours of energy, while the human brain operates on a continuous 20 watts.
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) counter this energy demand with a frugal approach and bring elements
of self-organization into the methodological repertoire of AI: local learning instead of global
error backpropagation, event-driven computing, and time as a carrier of information. They do not
replace today's AI but extend it; in hybrid systems each approach can be deployed where its
strengths lie. This monograph explains the field in a generally accessible yet mathematically
rigorous way. Using the text-generation module of ChatGPT as an example, the principle, elegance,
and computational cost of transformer-based AI are presented and compared with the coding, learning,
and network-design methods of SNNs. Throughout, relevant mechanisms of the human nervous system
serve as model and reference point. Topics include spike-timing-dependent plasticity, population and
temporal coding, the Neural Engineering Framework, neuromorphic hardware from Loihi 2 to commercial
edge processors, and the prospects for classical and neuromorphic AI to complement and extend each
other.
