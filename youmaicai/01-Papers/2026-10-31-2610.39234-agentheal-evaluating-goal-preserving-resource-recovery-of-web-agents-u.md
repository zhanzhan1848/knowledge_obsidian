## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Large language model (LLM) agents have demonstrated strong capabilities in automating complex
information-seeking and transactional tasks across the open web. However, their real-world
reliability remains limited by access barriers, such as dead links, authentication walls, rate
limits, and anti-scraping defenses. These barriers block the primary execution path, causing agents
to abort prematurely, loop indefinitely, or quietly compromise the user's original constraints.
Existing evaluations either implicitly assume that target resources are freely accessible or focus
on transient perturbations that simple retries can resolve, failing to capture scenarios where
agents must actively reroute around impassable bottlenecks. To bridge this gap, this paper
introduces AgentHeal, a comprehensive benchmark evaluating goal-preserving resource recovery,
defined as the ability of web agents to detect non-transient access barriers, pivot to legitimate
alternative sources, and fulfill the user's exact original objective. Spanning nine barrier families
across a counterfactual sandbox and real-website replay, our experiments on leading LLM backbones
show that average task success drops sharply from 89.4% to 43.7% under access barriers. Further
analysis reveals that agents frequently fall for deceptive look-alikes, fail to escalate to users
when necessary, violate access policies, or waste computation on futile retries, providing
actionable diagnostics for developing resilient web agents.
