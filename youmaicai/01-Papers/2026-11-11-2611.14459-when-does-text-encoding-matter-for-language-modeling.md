## 摘要（原文）
UTF-8's role as the de facto encoding standard in language models introduces cross-lingual
inequities due to its variable-length design. However, the effect that this encoding choice has on
model performance remains largely unexamined. In this work, we investigate how text encoding impacts
the performance of byte-level language models, comparing UTF-8 with a wide range of other encoding
strategies, including other Unicode-based approaches, language-specific encodings, and our own
customized encodings. To facilitate our analysis, we propose Aether: a hybrid encoding scheme that
uses script-specific encodings for our target setting while maintaining full Unicode coverage
through UTF-8 fallbacks, using this framework to model four orthographically diverse languages.
Surprisingly, we find that while languages with information-dense characters (Chinese, Korean)
significantly benefit from compressive encodings, languages whose characters carry less information
individually (Georgian, Tamil) are largely insensitive to encoding choice. In light of these
findings, we argue that text encoding should be a language-specific design decision, rather than a
universal default.
