## 核心要点
1. 提出 **"可解析的不充分性"（resolvable insufficiency）** 这一新情形：检索到的段落准确且相关，但给出的粒度比问题要求更粗（如问题问"家族性高脂血症"，段落只说"高脂血症"）。这类文本本身无法定出细粒度答案，但其提到的上位类别在领域本体中可映射到一个短的候选答案列表，因而不同于以往"上下文不充分"的简单判定。
2. 命名并刻画 **CLADE 现象**（Category-Level Answering from Deficient Evidence）：读到这类"仅含上位类别"的段落时，模型倾向于**直接作答而不弃答或继续检索**。在 PrimeKG 生物医学问答上，"仅类别"段落相比无信息段落使弃答率下降 **13.8 个百分点**，且这些猜测约有一半是错的；反事实实验进一步验证因果——把精确疾病名替换为其上位家族名，比直接删除该句使弃答率下降 **17.4 个百分点**。
3. 常见补救手段全部失效：**置信度过滤、扩大模型规模、显式指示模型弃答**都无法消除 CLADE，该现象在 GloBI 生态物种相互作用数据上同样复现。引入本体的修复路径部分有效：给定正确家族，本体候选列表在 **98.5%** 的情形下包含真实答案，但字符串匹配仅能在 **19.8%** 的页面中找到该家族；让模型自己选家族把这一比例提升到 **30.0%**，端到端恢复率从 **10.0%** 提升到 **26.8%**。

## 摘要（原文）
Retrieval-augmented systems often retrieve passages that are accurate and relevant but state the answer
at a coarser level of granularity than the question requires. For example, a question may ask which
specific disease a drug treats (familial hyperlipidemia), while the retrieved passage mentions the broader
disease family (hyperlipidemia). Prior work on context sufficiency treats such coarse passages simply as
insufficient. We argue that they form a distinct case, which we call resolvable insufficiency: text alone
cannot settle the fine-grained answer, but the broader category it mentions maps to a short list of candidate
answers in a domain ontology. When language models read these coarse, family-only passages, they exhibit a
phenomenon we call Clade (Category-Level Answering from Deficient Evidence): they answer instead of
abstaining or searching further. On biomedical questions from PrimeKG, family-only passages reduce abstention
by 13.8 points compared to uninformative passages, and the resulting guesses are wrong about half the time. A
counterfactual test confirms the cause: replacing an exact disease name with its broader family name lowers
abstention by 17.4 points compared to deleting the sentence. Simple fixes like confidence filtering, scaling
to a larger model, and explicitly instructing the model to abstain cannot resolve Clade, and this replicates on
ecological species interactions in GloBI. Finally, we test whether an ontology can turn coarse passages into
exact answers. Given the correct family, an ontology list includes the true answer in 98.5% of cases, but
string matching finds that family on the page for only 19.8%. Letting the model choose the family raises this
to 30.0% and end-to-end recovery from 10.0% to 26.8%. Overall, our results show when language models should
stop guessing and consult an ontology, and highlight which steps of that process remain unsolved.
