## 技术方案

### 核心思想

The last year has seen a shift in AI-assisted mathematics, with frontier language models producing
proofs of and counterexamples to long-standing open conjectures. However, studying only the current
set of major breakthroughs provides an incomplete picture of LLM capabilities and the computational
resources needed to achieve reported results. To address this, we introduce a new dataset,
OpenConjecture, which collects conjectures from mathematics papers published on the arXiv since
February 2026. We use these conjectures as a source of open mathematical problems, and apply
frontier GPT models to a random sample of 200 of these conjectures over three passes using a
reproducible protocol without problem-specific mathematical guidance. Rather than attempt expert
verification of these candidate solutions, we analyze the types of problems for which LLMs produce
solutions and the characteristics of these solutions. The models we test produce candidate solutions
for roughly 60% of the conjectures, with counterexamples about 1.3 times more common than proofs. We
estimate solution novelty, assess how solutions cite and relate to existing literature, and analyze
whether the proposed resolution relies on either an error in or underspecification of the
conjecture. We find that models sometimes propose resolutions that are outside the authors' intent
(e.g., proposing the trivial group as a counterexample) and that a number of proofs follow closely
from existing results, but that some resolutions exhibit at least moderate novelty. Our findings
underscore the need for evaluations that go beyond correctness to assess fidelity to the intended
statement, attribution, and the nature of the mathematical contribution.
