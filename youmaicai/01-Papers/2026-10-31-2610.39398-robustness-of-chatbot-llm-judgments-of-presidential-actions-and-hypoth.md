## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Chatbots are increasingly consulted about public affairs, and their judgments of policies are useful
only if they are robust. We measure this robustness in the setting of an ordinary user, through
consumer chat interfaces. For 30 days, five chatbot configurations (Mistral, Gemini Flash, Claude
Sonnet, and Claude Opus at two effort settings) were asked twice a day to score the long-term effect
on the United States of 40 actions of the last four presidential administrations and of 15
hypothetical reforms, each with a confidence and a short comment. The judgments are robust: all
configurations agree on the sign and the ranking of the administrations' mean scores, and two
different configurations asked on the same day give opposite signs to the same action in about 4% of
cases. Responses from different days differ more than same-day responses, but the difference grows
slowly over the month, except where the service itself changed. We identify three such changes,
which shifted the judgments while leaving little or no trace in the interface. Treating the comments
as a retrieval task, we find them specific to each item and consistent across runs and chatbots,
with lexical and embedding similarities agreeing. We release the dataset of 16,500 judgments.
