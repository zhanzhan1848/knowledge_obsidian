## 核心要点
1. 提出 **TIGA（Taxonomy Induction with Tool-Grounded Agents）**：把分类体系构建**形式化为显式状态 + 受约束动作空间上的迭代决策过程**，以保证层级结构完整、关系合法、组织原则一致，替代易漏实体/幻觉实体的自由生成式迭代。
2. 三个核心组件：**可执行的分类体系编辑工具**（tool grounding）、**记录工具调用、理由与结果的交互记忆（interaction memory）**、以及 agent 在**推理过程中自行修订的运行时自适应组织策略（runtime-adaptive policy）**；另设可选的**全局评审器**对照候选实体集与当前策略做审查。
3. 在 **4 个基准分类体系、2 个 LLM 后端**上与单次/迭代 prompting 基线对比：TIGA 在多数配置下取得**最强的结构质量**结果，但**并非在每个 precision 指标上都占优**；代价是**token 消耗、API 调用量与推理时间显著增加**。消融显示 policy 与 interaction-memory 组件带来一致收益，而全局评审的效果**依赖数据集与可用修订预算**。

## 摘要（原文）
Taxonomies are structured knowledge representations that support semantic search, recommendation,
product catalog organization, and retrieval-augmented generation. Their automated construction from a
fixed set of candidate entities requires more than plausible text generation: the resulting hierarchy must
be complete, structurally valid, and semantically coherent. Large Language Models (LLMs) are promising for
this task, but iterative free-form generation remains vulnerable to omitted or hallucinated entities,
invalid hierarchical relations, and inconsistent organizational principles. We introduce TIGA (Taxonomy
Induction with Tool-Grounded Agents), a knowledge-engineering architecture that formulates taxonomy
induction as an iterative decision process over an explicit state and constrained action space. TIGA
combines executable taxonomy-editing tools, an memory that records tool calls, rationales, and outcomes,
and a runtime-adaptive organizational policy that the agent revises during inference. An optional global
reviewer evaluates the constructed taxonomy against the candidate set and the active policy. We evaluate
TIGA on four benchmark taxonomies using two LLM backbones and compare it with single-shot and iterative
prompting-based baselines. TIGA achieves the strongest structural-quality results in most evaluated
configurations, but does not dominate on every precision measure. The gains are accompanied by
substantially higher token consumption, API-call volume, and inference time. Ablation results show
consistent benefits from the policy and interaction-memory components, whereas the effect of global review
depends on the dataset and the available revision budget. These findings characterize tool grounding and
runtime policy adaptation as promising but costly mechanisms for improving the controlled construction of
structured knowledge representations.
