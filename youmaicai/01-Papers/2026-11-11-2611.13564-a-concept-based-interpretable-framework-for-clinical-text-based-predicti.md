## 核心要点
1. 概念化解释通常需要标注的概念标签，而在临床文本中昂贵。
2. 提出**定制的 cross-attention 瓶颈**：临床文本与医学概念的文本描述先由预训练语言模型分词嵌入；cross-attention 模块估计**每个文本 token 上每个概念的存在性**，产出**概念相关性分数**；池化后送入线性预测层。
3. 在两个任务（私有胸部创伤严重度分层 + MIMIC-III 院内死亡预测）上 **AUROC 距对应黑箱模型在 0.02 以内**。
4. 同时支持**队列级与患者级**解释（整体概念重要性与患者特定概念贡献）。

## 摘要（原文）
Objective: Concept-based interpretation has emerged as a promising approach for explaining blackbox
models using human-understandable, high-level concepts. However, most existing methods require
annotated concept labels to train the model to identify concepts in the input, and obtaining such
annotations in clinical text is often expensive. This study aims to enable concept-based
interpretation for language models without concept supervision on clinical prediction tasks.
Methods: We propose a novel framework that enables concept-based interpretation without concept
supervision through a tailored cross-attention bottleneck. Clinical text and the text descriptions
of medical concepts are first tokenized and embedded by a pretrained language model. A cross-
attention module then estimates the presence of each concept for each text token, producing concept
relevance scores that capture how concepts are involved throughout the note. These scores are pooled
and used in a linear prediction layer to model clinical outcomes in an interpretable manner.
Results: The performance of the proposed framework was compared against blackbox models across two
tasks: chest trauma severity stratification using a private dataset and an in-hospital mortality
prediction task using the publicly available MIMIC-III dataset. The proposed framework achieved
AUROC values within 0.02 of the corresponding blackbox models. The framework also enabled cohort-
and patient-level interpretation, capturing the overall concept importance across the cohort and
patient-specific concept contributions. Conclusion: Our proposed framework realizes concept-based
interpretation without requiring ground-truth concept presence labels. It improves the transparency
of clinical text predictions while maintaining strong performance in clinical prediction tasks.
