## 摘要（原文）
Transformer sentence encoders provide strong retrieval quality, but their inference cost becomes a
bottleneck when embeddings must be produced on CPUs or refreshed frequently. We introduce StaRSE, a
compact Russian static sentence encoder that removes Transformer inference at deployment time: token
directions are stored as sign bits, token norms are stored separately, and sentences are encoded by
mean pooling over norm-scaled sign directions. StaRSE is initialized from ruBERT-base, fine-tuned on
Russian adjacent-sentence and task-shaped pairs, and post-trained with a contrastive stage that
directly optimizes the sign-projected token table. On RuMTEB, StaRSE reaches a task mean of $51.16$,
$7.98$ points above the strongest included static baseline. The packaged model occupies $11.3$ MiB
and has a mean CPU latency of $0.08$ ms per sentence, compared with an estimated $55.7$ MiB 16-bit
parameter footprint and $1.43$ ms mean CPU latency for the closest-quality contextual encoder.
Ablations examine adjacent-sentence supervision, token norms, and direct sign-projected post-
training.
