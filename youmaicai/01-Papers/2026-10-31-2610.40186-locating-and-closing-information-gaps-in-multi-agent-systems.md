## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Multi-step Web tasks often require several LLM agents to gather and combine evidence. The
communication graph of a fixed workflow restricts which agents can read each other's outputs:
evidence retrieved by one agent may never reach the agent that needs it, leaving an information gap
and an incomplete or incorrect answer. Adding a connection can deliver the missing evidence, but can
also lead the receiver to adopt an incorrect upstream conclusion that displaces a correct answer;
the final output alone does not separate the two. We propose Selective Probing and Link Insertion
for Closing information gaps in multi-agent Execution (SPLICE), which locates information gaps per
query and decides what to adopt after adding an edge. After the original workflow runs, SPLICE uses
the query and node outputs to locate a possible gap, adds one directed edge from a source to a
receiver to deliver the relevant evidence, and re-executes the receiver and affected downstream
nodes, producing a candidate answer. The adoption decision reads both the final answers of the two
runs and the process changes the edit caused inside the workflow: how far the receiver moved, where
its new content came from, and whether the change reached the final answer. A selector trained on
these process features chooses between adopting the candidate answer and retaining the original. On
five benchmarks, SPLICE raises the task score on Web tool use by 8.0% and the mean score on four
reasoning and planning benchmarks by 5.7% relative to the static skeleton, at 1.25 to 1.44$\times$
the skeleton's cost, and transfers zero-shot to four unseen backbones.
