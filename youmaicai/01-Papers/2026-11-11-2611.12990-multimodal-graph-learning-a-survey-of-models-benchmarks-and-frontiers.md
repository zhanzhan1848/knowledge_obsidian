## 摘要（原文）
Multimodal attributed graphs (MAGs), in which nodes carry text, images, and other modalities while
edges encode relations among entities, underlie e-commerce, social media, content platforms, and
creative applications. Learning on MAGs has developed along two complementary lines: graph-centric
methods redesign how modalities are fused, propagated, and connected within message passing, while
foundation-model-centric methods pretrain transferable multimodal graph encoders or bring graph
context into multimodal large language models for prediction and generation. In parallel, several
benchmarks have appeared with different datasets and evaluation protocols. This survey takes an
algorithm-centric and evaluation-grounded view of the field and makes three contributions. Modeling:
we introduce a unified message-passing formulation and organize methods into five integration levels
(feature, propagation, topology, learning, and foundation model), covering discriminative and graph-
conditioned generative models; within this formulation we derive representative methods as special
cases, compare their assumptions, expressiveness, and cost, and identify cross-modal sensitivity as
a property that separates otherwise similar architectures. Datasets and evaluation: we review recent
benchmarks, summarize their datasets and metrics, define model-independent descriptors (label
homophily, average degree, clustering, and edge provenance), and map each evaluation protocol to the
baselines it uses. Frontiers: we connect multimodal graph learning to AI agents, covering the
multimodal graphs that agents perceive and remember and graph engineering, the practice of
organizing agent workflows as explicit graphs; we then discuss open problems, concrete efforts for
the community, and research directions, including when graph structure helps in the presence of
strong multimodal foundation models.
