## 核心要点
1. **广义类别发现（GCD）**目标是从未标注数据中发现新类别，同时利用旧类别的少量标注样本。
2. 批评：仅依赖视觉线索的方法忽略人类感知的多模态本质；引入文本信息的方法通常把**全局标签与局部属性纠缠在共享的全局图像表示中**，使属性不可靠、可能提供误导线索。
3. **AptM-GCD**：**属性证据学习（AEL）** 把属性当作**局部化的视觉证据**而非全局类别描述符——属性嵌入与 patch 级视觉 token 交互，识别可靠的属性相关区域并抽取判别性局部证据，降低噪声属性线索的影响。
4. **多模态 prompt 学习（MPL）** 把细粒度文本证据转换为视觉 prompt 注入多模态 prompt transformer，连接局部属性语义与全局视觉理解。

## 摘要（原文）
Generalized category discovery (GCD) aims to discover novel categories from unlabeled data by
leveraging limited labeled samples from old classes. Existing methods that rely solely on visual
cues often neglect the multimodal nature of human perception, prompting recent approaches to
incorporate textual information such as category tags and attributes. However, these approaches
typically entangle global tags and local attributes within a shared global image representation,
rendering attributes unreliable and potentially providing misleading cues that can drive the model
toward incorrect predictions. To this end, we propose AptM-GCD, a novel multimodal framework that
exploits apt textual guidance through attribute evidence learning and multimodal prompt reasoning.
Specifically, we design an Attribute-Evidence Learning (AEL) mechanism that treats attributes as
localized visual evidence rather than global category descriptors. By interacting attribute
embeddings with patch-level visual tokens, AEL identifies reliable attribute-related regions and
extracts discriminative local evidence, thereby reducing the impact of noisy or irrelevant attribute
cues. Building upon the extracted evidence, we further develop a Multimodal Prompt Learning (MPL)
mechanism to bridge local attribute semantics and global visual understanding. MPL converts fine-
grained textual evidence into visual prompts and injects them into a multimodal prompt transformer,
enabling attribute information to enhance category discrimination through contextual reasoning
without enforcing direct global alignment between modalities. Extensive experiments on six datasets
demonstrate that AptM-GCD consistently achieves SoTA performance across both fine-grained and
generic benchmarks. Moreover, the proposed framework exhibits strong generalization ability,
delivering consistent gains under unimodal and class-imbalanced GCD settings.
