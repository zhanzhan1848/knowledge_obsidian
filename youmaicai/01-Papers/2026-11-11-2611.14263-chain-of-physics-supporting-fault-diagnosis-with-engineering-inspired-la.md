## 核心要点
1. **Chain of Physics (CoP)** 围绕一个**从资产专属参考文档自动生成的约束知识图谱（CKG）**构建，CKG 存储连接**失效模式、物理量变化与监测传感器**的诊断链。
2. 推理时：为每个问题检索语义相似的图路径，**同时作为信息与 in-context 示例**；候选失效模式从问题中提取并通过**独立的 LLM 调用**分析，生成可能的失效-观测链；最终决策模块比较这些链与可选答案。
3. **形式化物理要求被独立验证**。
4. 在 **FailureSensorIQ** 基准上跨四类工业资产与三个 LLM：相比直接提示提升 **7.8–19.6 pp**，相比常规 **RAG** 提升 **11.7–18.2 pp**。
5. 核心主张：**当图检索与失效传播的 in-context 示例结合时，结构化工程知识能改善 LLM 诊断推理。**

## 摘要（原文）
Fault diagnosis in complex systems requires linking observed changes in physical quantities to the
failure mechanisms that produce them. Experienced engineers perform this task by combining sensor
information with knowledge of system behavior and fault propagation. Large Language Models (LLMs)
can support this process, but their diagnostic reasoning depends on the availability of asset-
specific engineering knowledge and on how this knowledge is represented and used during inference.
This work introduces Chain of Physics (CoP), an LLM-based fault-diagnosis framework built around a
Constrained Knowledge Graph (CKG) generated automatically from asset-specific reference
documentation. CoP addresses failure-sensor association from textual engineering knowledge. The CKG
stores diagnostic chains connecting failure modes, changes in physical quantities, and monitoring
sensors. During inference, semantically similar graph paths are retrieved for each question and used
as both information and in-context examples for the reasoning process. Candidate failure modes are
extracted from the question and analysed independently through dedicated LLM calls, which generate
plausible failure-to-observation chains. A final decision module compares these chains with the
available answer choices. CoP is evaluated on the FailureSensorIQ benchmark across four industrial
asset classes and three LLMs. Compared with direct prompting and conventional Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG), CoP improves diagnostic accuracy across all evaluated models, with gains ranging
from 7.8 to 19.6 percentage points and from 11.7 to 18.2 percentage points, respectively. The
results show that structured engineering knowledge can improve LLM-based diagnostic reasoning when
graph retrieval is combined with in-context examples of fault propagation.
