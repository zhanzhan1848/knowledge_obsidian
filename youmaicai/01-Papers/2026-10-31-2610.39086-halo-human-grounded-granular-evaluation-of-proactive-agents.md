## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Proactive agents must decide whether, when, and how far to assist without an explicit request.
Existing evaluations often rely on automated scores or binary verdicts, offering limited human
diagnosis of evidence use and intervention quality. To address these gaps, we introduce HALO, Human-
Grounded GrAnular EvaLuation of PrOactive Agents. HALO-Arena covers 4 proactive need types,
Dependency, Blockage, Risk, and Opportunity, across 10 everyday domains and 50 personal
environments. Scenarios require agents to retrieve evidence from structured databases or
unstructured documents before judging whether assistance is warranted and choosing its scope. On
HALO-Arena, we construct HALO-8K, a dataset of 8,000 responses from 10 models across 400 scenarios,
annotated by 20 experts for action level, acceptability verdicts, and human diagnosis. For rejected
responses, human diagnosis covers timing, intensity, and other issues. Recorded reasoning tokens and
retrieval rounds measure the effort behind these responses. The best proprietary and open-source
models achieve 72.88% and 56.63% acceptability, respectively. Our central finding is that over-
proactivity is the dominant observed failure mode, with Premature timing or Excessive intensity
accounting for 61.20% of rejected responses. Further analyses yield complementary findings on the
balance between intervention and restraint, evidence acquisition, and the calibration of timing and
intensity.
