## 摘要（原文）
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated considerable potential in biomedical applications,
while agentic memory systems have emerged as a promising approach to preserving and utilizing
information across tasks and interactions. However, a fundamental question remains: how effectively
and reliably do these systems retain, retrieve, update, and utilize biomedical information? In this
study, we systematically investigate agentic memory in the biomedical domain, focusing on task
effectiveness, retrieval quality, information fidelity, and long-term robustness. We evaluate five
memory approaches across four LLMs in three representative settings: biomedical knowledge reasoning,
clinical prediction, and long-term conversational memory. Our analyses reveal how memory
construction, evidence relevance, and conversational history shape downstream biomedical
performance, highlighting the distinct challenges of retrieving relevant information, preserving its
fidelity, and effectively utilizing it for reasoning. Building on these findings, we introduce
Hybrid Memory Retrieval and Utilization (HMRU), a training-free framework that jointly enhances
evidence accessibility and task-aware utilization to improve biomedical reasoning and long-term
conversational memory. HMRU achieves an average relative improvement of 8.6% across biomedical
question-answering and conversational memory benchmarks, with additional gains in selected clinical
prediction tasks. It further reduces conversational answer errors by 24.9% and closes 48.0% of the
performance gap to full-context inference. Our findings provide a systematic characterization of
biomedical agentic memory and practical insights into developing more effective and reliable memory-
augmented LLM systems.
