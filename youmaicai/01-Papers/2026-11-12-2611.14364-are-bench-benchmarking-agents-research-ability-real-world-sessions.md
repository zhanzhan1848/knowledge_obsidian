## 核心要点
1. **基准动机与差异点**：现有研究 agent 基准多强调**独立任务**与**干净/策展过的 workspace**；但真实研究者要求 agent 在**进行中的项目**里完成一步，**请求依赖早先会话的决策**，workspace 里含**未完成文件、过时版本与失败尝试**。ARE-bench 基于**受聘研究者自己项目**的真实会话与完整 workspace 构建，检验 agent 能否推进在途项目并**尊重既有决策与现有工作区状态**。
2. **规模与构造**：共 **75 个任务**，覆盖**七个研究阶段**、**33 条需求**、**150 条子需求**，合计 **1,173 个二元 checklist 项**。每个任务锚定一个真实用户请求、保留其需求，并把**仍然生效的早期请求**总结为背景；**完整 workspace 被回放到该边界且不做清理**，保留早期轮次的所有产物；每个 checklist 由**意图对齐的 rubric** 编译而成，并对**任何有效解法**给分。
3. **关键结果与结论**：在**七个前沿模型**上，最强模型**仅在 66.2% 的任务上通过全部 checklist**，且 **Design 与 Execution 是所有模型都最难的两个阶段**。三个模型在只检查**交付契约与通用规范**时能通过约 **69%** 的任务，但一旦同时检查**历史性/物料性需求与 workspace 需求**，通过率骤降到 **28.4%–40.0%**——因此**忽略上下文需求的交付评估会高估研究 agent，并掩盖模型之间的差异**。

## 摘要（原文）
Research agents achieve increasingly strong benchmark scores, yet these scores do not necessarily translate
into reliable delivery in ongoing research projects. Many research-agent benchmarks emphasize standalone tasks
and relatively self-contained workflows in clean or curated workspaces. However, researchers ask agents to
complete one step within an ongoing project, where requests depend on earlier session decisions and
workspaces contain unfinished files, outdated versions, and failed attempts. We introduce ARE-bench to
evaluate whether research agents advance ongoing projects while respecting prior decisions and existing
workspace state, grounded in real session context and in-progress workspaces. ARE-bench includes 75 tasks
built on agent sessions and full workspaces from recruited researchers' own projects, spanning seven research
stages, 33 requirements, and 150 sub-requirements, with 1,173 binary checklist items in total. Each task is
anchored at a real user request, preserves its requirements, and summarizes the earlier requests still in effect
as background. The complete workspace is replayed to that boundary without cleanup, preserving artifacts from
earlier turns. Each checklist is compiled from an intent-aligned rubric and credits any valid solution. Across
seven frontier models, the strongest passes every checklist item on only 66.2% of tasks, and Design and
Execution rank among the hardest stages for every model. Three models pass about 69% of tasks when only
delivery contracts and general norms are checked, but just 28.4% to 40.0% once historical and material or
workspace requirements are also checked. Evaluating delivery without these contextual requirements can
therefore overestimate research agents and mask differences between them.
