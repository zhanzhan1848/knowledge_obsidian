## 技术方案

### 核心思想

We present TextSeal2, a distortion-free watermark for large language models that keeps its signal
under speculative decoding: tokens that replace rejected drafts remain watermarked with the same
keys, and the acceptance rate is unchanged. It also preserves output diversity and adds no
measurable inference latency. Its predecessor, TextSeal, restores diversity in the Gumbel-max scheme
by routing each token to one of two keys via a coin flip. This is suboptimal because the detector
averages the scores from both keys without knowing which key was used. TextSeal2 instead merges two
or more keys into a single virtual key and couples it to fresh noise through AutoMark's Gaussian
copula. Any number of keys, including publicly shared keys, can be merged this way without diluting
detection. Under speculative decoding, TextSeal2 verifies keyed drafts with one more exponential
race, run on the leftovers of the draft races. By memorylessness, this race reproduces block
verification, so the output follows the target model exactly. We evaluate TextSeal2 on three LLMs.
At equal output token diversity, TextSeal2 detects 8 to 22 percentage points more 100-token answers
than TextSeal at a false-positive rate of $10^{-3}$, and is comparable to textGrain while requiring
no post-logits optimization. Under speculative decoding, it detects 18 and 22 percentage points more
200-token answers than TextSeal and textGrain, respectively, at the same acceptance rate.
