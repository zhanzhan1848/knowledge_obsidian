## 摘要（原文）
Adding abstention to a task-adapted multimodal model can discard correct answers, including
capabilities acquired during adaptation. We formulate abstention repair as maximizing correct
abstention on unanswerable questions under a budget on newly lost correct answers. We propose
ReFrain (Regression-Free Abstention), which combines a linear probe on first-token logits, threshold
calibration on held-out image clusters, and a deterministic veto that replaces rejected outputs with
the displayed "none of the above" option while preserving all other responses. "Regression-free"
denotes zero loss on the calibration set at the zero-budget target. All abstention methods in our
main comparison start from the same answering-SFT checkpoint; we separately track lost correct
answers, repaired errors, and retained SFT gains. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, ReFrain at zero budget retains
all 57 SFT-gained answers with no losses and improves correct abstention by 7.30 percentage points.
At a 0.5% calibration budget, it reaches 74.05% correct abstention with 8 losses and retains 55
gains; CLIP-UP EmbLoRA reaches 64.65% with 62 losses, 41 repairs, and 40 retained gains. The same
probe at its default threshold loses 298 correct answers. With models and thresholds frozen,
confirmation on 228 independent question clusters yields 66.05% correct abstention and no losses at
the 0.5% point, exceeding EmbLoRA by 32.72 percentage points. Ordinary refusal SFT achieves higher
correct abstention at greater regression cost. Code and reproduction materials:
https://github.com/ModeLiberaL/ReFrain.
