## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Cyber threat intelligence (CTI) reports describe adversary behavior in natural language, but
converting them into structured MITRE ATT&CK mappings remains difficult: existing methods decompose
reports to sentence-level granularity for extraction, while modern LLMs can work with full reports
in a single pass but with noticeable performance degradation. We present ARBITRA, which orchestrates
multiple role-specialized LLM experts that propose candidate ATT&CK entities from the full report,
which are then verified through a per-candidate quantitative bipolar argumentation framework (QBAF)
grounded in document evidence and rationales. We additionally construct reasoning-specialized
corpora for fine-tuning so experts can act as effective participants during this verification
process. Across ATT&CK extraction benchmarks, ARBITRA reaches a stronger precision-oriented
operating point than sentence-level pipelines, recent LLM-based extraction systems, and frontier
models including GPT-5.5. Under adversarial perturbations simulating conflicting TTP statements,
cross-report contamination, and semantic poisoning, ARBITRA limits mean precision degradation to
roughly half that of frontier-model baselines for a more robust extraction.
