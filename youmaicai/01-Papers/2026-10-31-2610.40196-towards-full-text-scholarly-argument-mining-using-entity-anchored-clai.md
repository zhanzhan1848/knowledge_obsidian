## 技术方案

### 核心思想

Argument mining in full-text scholarly articles remains an important yet challenging task. This
difficulty stems not only from the need to model long-distance dependencies but also from the
substantial effort required to annotate sentence-level argumentative components throughout lengthy
documents. To address these challenges, we introduce a novel approach that leverages scientific
entity mentions (e.g., tasks, datasets, and models) to identify and structure claims in scholarly
literature. We validate our approach through a case study involving 50 full-text research papers on
sentiment analysis. We first establish a reliable human gold standard (Krippendorff's $α= 0.80$),
identifying 113 claim-bearing sentences and extracting 199 structured claim records from 300
candidate sentences. To address the annotation bottleneck, we employ a majority-voting ensemble of
large language models (LLMs) to generate a silver-standard dataset comprising 1,487 claims. Encoders
fine-tuned exclusively on the silver-standard dataset outperform those trained on a small human-
annotated gold-standard dataset and demonstrate strong zero-shot transfer to NLP papers outside the
training corpus.
