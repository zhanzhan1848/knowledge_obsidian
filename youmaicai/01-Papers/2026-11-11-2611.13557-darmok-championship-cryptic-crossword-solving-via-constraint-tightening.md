## 核心要点
1. **第 55 届 Times Crossword 锦标赛**（伦敦）与人类选手同场竞技：七道题**全部零错**，且**每一轮都快于所有人类选手**。
2. 决赛题 **6m52s**，比人类冠军快 **3m13s**。
3. 可靠性设计（三重冗余 + 交叉验证）：**三模型交叉检查线索**、**用已验证的交叉字母解锁更难的线索**、**用整题模型与词典补空**（且不覆盖已确认词）。
4. 在**五个月的每日评测**中维持近零误差。
5. 作者同时泼冷水：今天 frontier 模型平均一次尝试即可解整题、速度与准确率相当，**但云 API 调用仍会卡顿、超时、触发安全拒答或陷入推理循环**——因此冗余架构是必要的。

## 摘要（原文）
We present Darmok, the first automated system to reach superhuman performance on British cryptic
crosswords. Unlike American-style crosswords, cryptic clues disguise an unmarked definition and
compositional wordplay beneath a misleading surface reading, sparse grids leave nearly half of each
word's letters unchecked, and tournament rules rank a grid with even a single wrong or blank square
behind all error-free solutions regardless of speed. Competing alongside an elite human field at the
55th Times Crossword Championship in London, Darmok solved all seven championship puzzles error-free
and finished faster than every human competitor in every round. It solved the Final puzzle in 6m
52s, a full 3m 13s ahead of the human champion. Today's frontier AI models can on average solve a
whole puzzle in a single try with comparable accuracy and speed. However, cloud API calls still
stall, time out, trigger safety refusals, or get stuck in reasoning loops. To simultaneously achieve
tournament-grade accuracy, speed, and reliability, Darmok cross-checks clues across three
independent models in rounds; uses verified crossing letters to unlock harder clues; and uses a
whole-puzzle model and dictionary lookups to fill any remaining gaps without overwriting confirmed
words. This design maintained near-zero-error performance across a five-month daily evaluation
period. Under normal benchmark conditions, removing backup models, fallback steps, or elaborate
prompt instructions barely changes accuracy, making the architecture appear over-engineered. Yet
during both the worst days of daily operation and a severe slowdown of our primary model in the live
Championship Final, the backup paths carried the solve, showing that as foundation models master the
average case, the central challenge in building complex AI systems shifts to resilience in the worst
case.
