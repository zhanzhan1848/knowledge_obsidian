## 摘要（原文）
The Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC) is a benchmark designed to assess the capability of
Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems to generalize starting from a few samples, a task humans excel
at. The ARC-AGI contests held since 2020 have been the focus of intense competition, with
participants proposing approaches ranging from traditional symbolic AI to Transformer architectures.
Despite a long history of successes, evolutionary algorithms (EAs) were notably absent from the
approaches appearing in the main contest. As score-and-search program synthesis approaches, EAs
suffer from issues related to choosing the proper representation in the Domain-Specific Language
applied to the problem (for example, pixel-level or object-level), and distinguishing between two
candidate solutions producing the same outputs. In this work, we present the first fully symbolic,
evolutionary-based program synthesis approach submitted to the ARC-AGI-2 main contest. The main
contributions are: evolving the grid parser and the output projector together with the program that
transforms the grid, so that a task-specific language emerges during program synthesis; grounding
the symbols the parser can extract in core knowledge theory from cognitive psychology; and an
object-aware fitness that grades partially correct outputs beyond pixel-level discrepancies.
